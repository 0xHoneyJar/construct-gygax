# Game Traditions

Gygax analyzes 20 game traditions. The analytical tools are the same; the tradition determines how they're applied — which entity types matter most, which heuristics to load, which scripts to invoke for resolution math.

## TTRPGs

| Tradition | Slug | Examples | Distinctive |
|-----------|------|----------|-------------|
| **d20** | `d20` | D&D, Pathfinder, 13th Age | Ability scores, AC, HP, attack rolls, saving throws, spell slots, CR, levels |
| **Powered by the Apocalypse** | `pbta` | Dungeon World, Apocalypse World, Masks, Monsterhearts | Stats -1 to +3, 2d6+stat moves, 10+/7-9/6- outcomes, playbooks, harm/conditions |
| **Forged in the Dark** | `fitd` | Blades in the Dark, Scum and Villainy | Action ratings, position/effect, stress/trauma, clocks, crew sheets |
| **OSR** | `osr` | OSE, Knave, Cairn, Into the Odd | Minimal stats, high lethality, procedures over rules, reaction tables, resource depletion |
| **Cepheus / Traveller** | `cepheus` | Traveller, Cepheus Engine | 2d6+mod vs 8+, Effect-based gradient, lifepath careers, 6 characteristics, unskilled -3 penalty |
| **Freeform** | `freeform` | Wanderhome, Belonging Outside Belonging | Minimal resolution, fiction-first, safety tools as mechanics |
| **Custom** | `custom` | Journaling, GMless, map-drawing, lyric games | Anything truly experimental |

## How Tradition Shapes Analysis

The tradition field in `index.yaml` flows through every skill.

### `/attune` Extraction

Different traditions emphasize different entity types. Attune adjusts what it looks for in source material:

| Tradition | Primary Entities | Often Sparse |
|-----------|-----------------|--------------|
| d20 | stats, mechanics, progression | tensions |
| pbta | mechanics (moves), stats | resources, tensions |
| fitd | resources (stress), mechanics (actions, position/effect) | stats, tensions |
| osr | mechanics (procedures), entities (monsters, treasure), resources (supply) | progression, tensions |
| cepheus | stats (6 characteristics), mechanics (skill checks, Effect), progression (lifepath) | tensions |
| freeform | mechanics (tokens, prompts), tensions (safety tools as mechanics) | stats, resources, progression |
| eurogame | mechanics (actions), resources (economy), entities (components) | stats |
| deckbuilder | mechanics (buy/play/trash), entities (cards), resources (currency) | stats, tensions |
| autobattler | entities (units), mechanics (combat, synergies), resources (economy) | stats, tensions |
| roguelike | mechanics (run structure), progression (meta-unlocks), entities (items, builds) | stats, tensions |
| social-deduction | entities (roles), mechanics (phases, voting), tensions (information asymmetry) | stats, progression |
| idle | mechanics (click/automate loop), progression (prestige), resources (currencies) | stats, entities |

For **custom** games, attune lets the game decide. A journaling RPG may have only `mechanics/` (prompts) and `tensions/` (thematic). A GMless game may have no `entities/` at all. Empty categories are better than forced ones.

### `/augury` Resolution Math

The probability scripts invoked depend on the resolution method:

- **Single d20**: `dice-probability.ts`
- **2d6, 3d6, Traveller/Cepheus sums**: `bell-curve.ts`
- **Dice pools** (Shadowrun, FitD, Storyteller): `dice-pool.ts`
- **Advantage/disadvantage** (5e-style): `advantage.ts`
- **Exploding/acing dice** (Savage Worlds, DCC, Feng Shui): `exploding-dice.ts`
- **Non-dice methods** (auction, worker-placement, simultaneous-choice, deterministic, deduction): structural analysis instead of probability

### `/cabal` Archetype Behavior

The same archetypes test the same player mindsets in every tradition, but with behavioral weightings adapted:

- The **Optimizer** in a TTRPG min-maxes a build. In a journaling RPG, it finds prompt sequences that produce the most dramatic entries.
- The **Rules Lawyer** in a d20 game finds RAW edge cases. In a GMless game, it probes authority distribution.
- The **Newcomer** in an autobattler asks how draft works. In a freeform game, it asks what a scene even is.

If an archetype genuinely can't engage with a game, that itself is a finding.

### `/lore` Heuristic Libraries

Each tradition has a curated heuristic library at `skills/lore/resources/{tradition}.yaml`:

- `d20.yaml` — linear fighter / quadratic wizard, MAD dependency, bounded accuracy violations
- `pbta.yaml` — move trigger overlap, missing miss consequences, playbook overshadowing
- `fitd.yaml` — stress economy mismatch, position/effect inflation, downtime starvation
- `osr.yaml` — combat-as-war vs sport, information-as-treasure, weight-of-choice

460+ heuristics across the 20 traditions. **Tradition-specific** heuristics fire only when the matching tradition is set. **Cross-tradition** heuristics (`tradition_specific: false`) fire everywhere.

For `custom` and `freeform`, lore loads all applicable libraries and falls back to structural analysis (decision space, loop completeness, tension health, pacing) when nothing matches. If fewer than 3 heuristics match, lore says so honestly.

## When Gygax Doesn't Know

> I will also tell you, clearly, when I don't know enough about a tradition or genre to have a strong opinion. I'd rather surface that gap than fill it with confident-sounding approximation.

Depth varies by tradition — TTRPG traditions are deepest, digital traditions are growing. For traditions with sparse heuristic libraries, findings carry a `[structural-only]` tag indicating that pattern-matching against tradition-specific precedent wasn't available.

If your game is a journaling RPG about playing as a lighthouse and none of the curated heuristics directly apply — that's a finding, not a reason to pretend the heuristics apply anyway. See the [`false_expertise` refusal](/identity/refusals).
